
Share buybacks are not inherently value-creating; their success depends entirely on capital allocation discipline and business durability. Mohnish Pabrai highlights two critical preconditions:

1. **No Secular Decline**: A company must not be undergoing structural or technological disruption. If a business's terminal earnings fall toward zero (e.g., Sears or IBM in certain eras), repurchasing shares merely concentrates ownership in a dying asset.
2. **Price vs. Intrinsic Value**: Buybacks create maximal value when executed at low earnings multiples or significant discounts to intrinsic value. Repurchasing stock at inflated multiples destroys shareholder capital.

Additionally, management teams must prioritize internal reinvestment at high return on equity (ROE) before allocating excess capital toward repurchases.
