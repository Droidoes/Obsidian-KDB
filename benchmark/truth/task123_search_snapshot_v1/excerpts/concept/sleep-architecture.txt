
Sleep architecture refers to the cyclical structural organization of sleep stages across a sleep period. Normal sleep cycles roughly every 90 minutes through Non-REM stages (N1, N2, and N3 or deep/delta sleep) and [[rem-sleep|REM sleep]]. Deep sleep occurs predominantly in the first half of the night and is characterized by large delta waves and slow metabolic rates, whereas REM sleep dominates the final third of the night with heightened brain activity and rapid eye movements. Human sleep naturally includes roughly 10 brief micro-awakenings per hour to maintain postural muscle tone and circulation. With advancing age, total sleep time decreases, wakefulness after sleep onset increases, and deep sleep drops significantly—particularly in men, who may retain less than 5% deep sleep by age 85.
