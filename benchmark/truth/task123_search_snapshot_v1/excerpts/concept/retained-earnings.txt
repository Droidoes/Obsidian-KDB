
Retained earnings represent the cumulative net income retained by a corporation rather than distributed to shareholders as dividends. Found under the [[shareholders-equity]] section of the balance sheet, this figure grows when a business generates profits and keeps a portion to reinvest in operations.

Consistent year-over-year growth in retained earnings—especially during broader economic downturns or recessions—serves as evidence that a business possesses durable competitive strength and profitable capital reinvestment opportunities.
