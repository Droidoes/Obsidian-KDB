
Margin of safety is the practice of purchasing assets at a substantial discount to their conservative intrinsic value to protect against downside risk and human error. As [[li-lu|Li Lu]] explains, because minority shareholders cannot control corporate management, demanding a large margin of safety serves as self-defense. Evaluating a margin of safety requires inspecting tangible assets—such as cash, liquid working capital, real estate, or marketable securities—and verifying that the entry price provides structural downside protection regardless of market volatility.
