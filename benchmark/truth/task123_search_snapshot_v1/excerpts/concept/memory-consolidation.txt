
Memory consolidation is the process by which the brain stabilizes and encodes newly acquired information during sleep. Research indicates that up to 80% of recall dependent on post-learning sleep quality. Specific sleep stages facilitate distinct types of learning: [[rem-sleep|REM sleep]] aids in abstract processing and grasping general concepts (useful for recognition and multiple-choice tasks), while deep delta sleep consolidates spatial and motor procedural learning, such as surgical techniques, musical practice, or athletic routines.
