
Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is a sleep disorder characterized by repetitive physical collapse or blockage of the upper airway during sleep, leading to severe oxygen desaturation, surges in blood pressure, and sleep fragmentation. Prevalent in 50% to 70% of older adults due to soft tissue laxity, OSA is a severe cardiovascular risk factor linked to hypertension, stroke, and increased 10-year mortality. The primary gold-standard treatment is Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP), alongside weight loss, positional therapies, myofunctional exercises, or oral appliance therapy.
