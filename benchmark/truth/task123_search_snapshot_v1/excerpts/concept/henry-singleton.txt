
Henry Singleton was the founder and CEO of Teledyne, widely regarded by Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger as one of the greatest capital allocators in corporate history. Singleton operated a distinct two-phase capital allocation strategy:

- **1960s (Equity Issuance & Conglomeration)**: When Teledyne traded at high price-to-earnings multiples (40x–70x), Singleton used Teledyne stock as currency to acquire 130 companies operating at lower multiples (~10x).
- **1970s–1980s (Aggressive Buybacks)**: When the bear market depressed Teledyne's P/E below 10, Singleton pivoted entirely. Between 1972 and 1984, he repurchased over 90% of Teledyne's outstanding shares, utilizing issuer tender offers and issuing 10% debentures in exchange for stock.

Singleton maintained decentralized operations by leaving acquired business units independent to preserve manager accountability and clear operational benchmarking.
