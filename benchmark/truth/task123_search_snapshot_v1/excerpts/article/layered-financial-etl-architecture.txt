
Raw financial statement processing often fails when treating financial reports as flat text lists. Financial statements are semantic trees where meaning depends on hierarchy, disclosure context, and accounting constraints. A robust architecture separates mapping recognition from semantic post-processing.

### 1. Ingestion and Mapping
Line items are normalized using a [[financial-etl-decision-tree]] that prioritizes structural context, decomposition rules, and explicit section constraints over simple string matching. This stage maps raw text labels to normalized canonical names.

### 2. Semantic Materialization and Reasoning
Once flat facts reside in storage, a [[canonical-financial-ontology]] acts as a semantic post-processing layer. The ontology explicitly defines accounting roles, rollups, and invariant constraints. It automatically materializes missing derived concepts without altering the raw ingestion pipeline.

### 3. Metric Derivation and Validation
Derived metrics like [[ontology-driven-ebitda]] fall out naturally from semantic roles rather than hardcoded company-specific logic. Subsequently, a [[financial-reconciliation-engine]] evaluates cross-statement integrity, while metrics such as [[canonical-value-coverage]] evaluate the economic completeness of the dataset. This layered separation ensures the system remains explainable, auditable, and resilient to varied corporate reporting standards.
