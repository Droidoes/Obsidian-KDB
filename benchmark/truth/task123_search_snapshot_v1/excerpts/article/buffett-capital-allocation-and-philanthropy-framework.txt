
Warren Buffett's approach to capital management applies the same fundamental principles to both investing and philanthropy. In business, he relies on a strict [[circle-of-competence]] to identify mispriced opportunities and acts decisively during [[market-panics]] when fear depresses security prices below their intrinsic value, securing a high [[margin-of-safety]]. In philanthropy, he applies Adam Smith's principle of specialization of labor: rather than building his own operational foundations, he delegates capital distribution to younger, specialized experts like the Gates Foundation. Through initiatives like the [[giving-pledge]], Buffett treats wealth allocation as a rational exercise in directing excess capital—which holds no personal utility for the billionaire—toward high-impact societal needs before death.
